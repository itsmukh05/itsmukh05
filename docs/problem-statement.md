# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Campus IT administrators who manage computing resources, researchers who study computing systems, and students who use campus computing services.

## What exactly is going wrong?
When many students submit computing jobs at the same time, limited server capacity may cause long waiting times and large queues. However, keeping too many servers active when demand is low may waste computing resources. Fixed-capacity allocation does not adjust to changing demand, while threshold-based and adaptive policies may respond differently to workload changes.

## Why does it matter?
Long waiting times can affect students who need to complete computing tasks, while inefficient server usage may increase operating costs and waste available resources. Comparing different resource allocation policies can help identify suitable ways to balance system performance and resource usage.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Campus IT administrators face challenges in managing computing resources when students submit different numbers of computing jobs at different times. When many jobs arrive together, limited server capacity may cause longer waiting times and larger queues, while keeping too many servers active during low demand may waste resources. Research on cloud computing autoscaling discusses the challenge of balancing resource availability and efficient resource usage (Jeong & Jeong, 2025). Therefore, this project will use simulation to compare fixed-capacity, threshold-based, and adaptive resource allocation policies under different workload conditions.
